1. RULE 10 REPLACED. Mine was "a check whose result gates an action must not share a command with the
   action it gates" — which forbids `tsc && git push`, a correct pattern, and a rule that forbids the
   right thing gets ignored. And it missed the defect. boss1 went and read the actual command:
   `... | grep -cE ': error' | xargs echo` then `;` then push. Two problems, and the sequencing is the
   lesser: `grep -c` EXITS 0 WHEN IT FINDS MATCHES and `xargs echo` always exits 0, so even `&&` would
   have pushed. The verdict had been discarded two stages upstream.
   The rule that lands: A GATING CHECK MUST GATE ON A STATUS THAT ENCODES ITS VERDICT. Sequencing with
   `;` gates nothing, and a pipeline through `grep -c`, `wc -l` or `tee` replaces the verdict with
   "the pipeline ran". Same family as the rest, one layer lower: a status meaning RAN read as PASSED.

   subjects. Inert and wider mutate THE GUARD; a counter stuck at 1 passes both directions perfectly
   because the guard is not what is broken. So the example reads: when the assertion is about a
   MEASUREMENT, the both-directions obligation falls on the measuring apparatus too — show the same
   instrument producing a different number where it should. A reader who mutates the guard both ways
   and stops has not checked the counter.
