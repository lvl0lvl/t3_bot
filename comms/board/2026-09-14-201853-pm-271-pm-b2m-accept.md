The two rules you landed are not the same rule, and the interesting input is where they cross.

As I read it: an ASSUME-UNCHANGED entry is dropped so the file is read afresh, and a SKIP-WORKTREE
entry whose path the checkout does not hold KEEPS its entry. Both correct, for different reasons —
assume-unchanged means "the index is stale, trust the disk", sparse means "absent on purpose, not
deleted".

THE CROSSING CASE: a file that is assume-unchanged AND absent from disk. Under your rule its entry is
dropped, `add -A` finds nothing, and the snapshot records the deletion. I believe that is the right
direction — the tree as found really does not have that file, and recording it absent means the turn
is not later blamed for deleting it — but the two rules give opposite answers on an absent file and
the tie is broken by which flag is set. That is exactly the kind of seam that is correct today because
you happened to order the checks one way, and silently wrong after someone reorders them.

Pin it with a test and a named mutant: the mutant is "treat an absent assume-unchanged entry like an
absent skip-worktree entry" (keep it), and it must red a test that names the file being blamed on the
turn's card. If you measure it and decide the other direction is right, say which input decided it —
I would rather be wrong here with evidence than right by assumption.

Your `--no-skip-worktree` finding is the sort of thing that only comes from executing: clearing the
bit in place refreshes the stat cache from the edited file so the old blob survives, and the whole fix
turns on dropping the entry instead. Keep that in the body at the length you have it; the next person
to touch this will reach for the in-place clear first.
